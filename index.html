My research examines how the spatial composition and visual qualities of urban and natural environments influence perception, emotional wellbeing and cognitive performance.

Integrating quantitative landscape analysis, immersive experiments and multimodal human-factor measurements, I investigate how environmental features shape visual attention, stress recovery and sustained attention, and translate these relationships into evidence-based design guidance.

My current work extends this approach from urban green spaces to long-duration polar habitats, exploring how repeated exposure and environmental novelty can inform adaptive visual environments that support attention and wellbeing.

I received my Ph.D. in Knowledge Science from the Japan Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (JAIST) in 2025, with a research focus on urban design. I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at Tongji University.

RESEARCH DIRECTION · PROPOSED STUDY

Environmental Novelty and Sustained Attention

How can visual environments remain supportive as they become familiar? This proposed study explores how environmental novelty affects sustained attention during repeated exposure, informing adaptive visual environments for long-duration polar habitation.
